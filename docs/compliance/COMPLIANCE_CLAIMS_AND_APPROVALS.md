## Claim tests

- [ ] International Organization for Standardization/International
  Electrotechnical Commission (ISO/IEC), System and Organization Controls 2
  (SOC 2), and General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) terminology is used
  accurately for the approved scope.
- [ ] The claim names the exact entity, product, service, system, period,
  standard/version, criteria, conformance level, or jurisdiction when material.
- [ ] The claim does not broaden point-in-time, sampled, repository, design,
  internal-review, or readiness evidence into period-long or system-wide assurance.
- [ ] “Certified” identifies the standard, scope, valid certificate, certification
  body, validity period, exclusions, and current status.
- [ ] “SOC 2” identifies the actual report scope, criteria, type, period or date,
  auditor, intended-use restrictions, exceptions, and current availability.
- [ ] “Compliant” is not inferred from an objective, roadmap, control mapping,
  automated scan, policy, checklist, prior release, vendor assertion, or percentage.
- [ ] Security, privacy, AI, accessibility, financial-crime, ethical-supply-chain,
  and sustainability statements preserve known limitations and open findings.
- [ ] Contractual questionnaires distinguish implemented, verified, inherited,
  planned, unavailable, not applicable, and confidential information.
- [ ] Public statements, filings, certificates, audit reports, attestations,
  suspicious-activity reports, and customer responses use the authorised form and signer.
- [ ] Expiry, withdrawal, incident, scope-change, evidence-staleness, and correction
  triggers are defined.

## Prohibited unsupported wording

Unless current, scoped evidence and the correct authority support it, do not use:

- “fully compliant”;
- “certified” or a certification logo;
- “SOC 2 compliant” or “SOC 2 certified”;
- “GDPR certified”;
- “zero risk,” “completely secure,” “breach-proof,” or “vulnerability-free”;
- “free from modern slavery” or “no money-laundering risk”;
- “approved by the regulator”;
- “independently audited” when only internal or AI review occurred; or
- wording that implies every release, repository, geography, service, or period
  is covered by evidence for a narrower scope.
