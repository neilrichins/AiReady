# Modern slavery and forced-labour due-diligence checklist

Use this optional record when the
[applicability decision](COMPLIANCE_APPLICABILITY_CHECKLIST.md) identifies
modern-slavery, forced-labour, human-trafficking, child-labour, supply-chain
transparency, human-rights due-diligence, procurement, or contractual concerns.

Requirements differ by entity, turnover, market, sector, supply chain, contract,
and jurisdiction. The United Kingdom government publishes current
[transparency-in-supply-chains guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/transparency-in-supply-chains-a-practical-guide)
and an optional reporting template designed to support organisations reporting
in the United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada. Verify each country's legislation,
guidance, mandatory content, approval, publication, and filing requirements.

This checklist does not prove that a business or supply chain is free of modern
slavery. Artificial intelligence (AI) and document review cannot establish
actual worker conditions alone.

## 1. Applicability and reporting perimeter

- [ ] Identify every legal entity, parent/subsidiary relationship, partnership,
  operation, market, public contract, customer flow-down, and group statement option.
- [ ] Determine turnover or other thresholds, territorial nexus, supply of goods
  or services, mandatory reporting period, filing, registry, website, approval,
  signature, publication, and update requirements.
- [ ] Identify separate Australian, Canadian, United Kingdom, European Union,
  national, state, procurement, import, customs, sanctions, and contractual duties.
- [ ] Determine which workers, labour providers, contractors, suppliers, products,
  services, hardware, facilities, data-labelling operations, content moderation,
  logistics, hosting, cleaning, security, and other operations are in scope.
- [ ] Record non-applicability and group reliance using current legal evidence and approval.

- [ ] Map beyond direct suppliers where risk and available leverage justify it.
- [ ] Identify labour recruiters, subcontractors, dormitories, transport,
  recruitment fees, migration routes, remote work, prisons, home work, temporary
  work, and state-imposed labour where relevant.
- [ ] Include digital supply chains such as device manufacture, mineral inputs,
  logistics, data annotation, content review, outsourced support, facilities,
  and cloud or colocation operations.
- [ ] Record unknown tiers and worker groups rather than treating lack of visibility as low risk.

## 3. Risk assessment

| Risk | Country/sector/product/workforce | Evidence and stakeholder input | Severity/likelihood | Existing controls | Residual risk | Action/owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `{{RISK}}` | `{{CONTEXT}}` | `{{EVIDENCE}}` | `{{ASSESSMENT}}` | `{{CONTROLS}}` | `{{LEVEL}}` | `{{ACTION}}` |

- [ ] Use authoritative country, sector, product, recruitment, migration, conflict,
  import, enforcement, civil-society, trade-union, worker, and supplier evidence.
- [ ] Assess severity to people, scale, scope, irremediability, likelihood,
  purchasing practices, leverage, visibility, and connection to the harm.
- [ ] Investigate unusually low prices, impossible lead times, recruitment fees,
  retained documents, movement restriction, debt, threats, withheld wages,
  excessive overtime, unsafe conditions, deceptive recruitment, and grievance suppression.
- [ ] Do not infer low risk solely from supplier self-attestation, country score,
  certification, audit pass, absence of reports, or AI sentiment analysis.
- [ ] Prioritise action according to risk to people, not reputational exposure alone.

## 4. Governance, policy, and purchasing practices

- [ ] Assign governing-body, executive, procurement, human-rights, legal,
  compliance, human-resources, operations, security, product, and remediation ownership.
- [ ] Approve policies covering prohibited practices, worker rights, recruitment
  fees, document retention, wages, freedom of movement, grievance, non-retaliation,
  subcontracting, due diligence, remediation, termination, and reporting.
- [ ] Align price, lead time, forecasting, specification changes, payment terms,
  sourcing incentives, and supplier performance measures with the policy.
- [ ] Include proportionate supplier-selection, contract, disclosure, access,
  worker-engagement, remediation, escalation, notification, and termination terms.
- [ ] Train relevant decision-makers and workers using appropriate languages,
  formats, roles, local context, and protected reporting routes.

## 5. Due diligence and worker evidence

- [ ] Perform risk-based supplier and site due diligence before onboarding and
  throughout the relationship, including ownership, labour model, recruitment,
  subcontracting, prior findings, and corrective-action history.
- [ ] Use announced and unannounced audits only as part of a broader evidence model.
- [ ] Seek safe, accessible, confidential worker and representative input without
  management interference, coached responses, retaliation, or unsafe data collection.
- [ ] Verify employment terms, payment, hours, age, identity-document control,
  freedom of movement, recruitment fees/debt, accommodation, grievance access,
  and subcontractor conditions where relevant.
- [ ] Protect worker, complainant, trade-union, investigation, and supplier data
  and limit disclosure according to safety, privacy, and legal requirements.
- [ ] Record evidence limitations and do not fabricate confidence where access is restricted.

## 6. Grievance, investigation, and remediation

- [ ] Provide trusted, accessible, multilingual, confidential, non-retaliatory
  grievance routes for workers and external stakeholders.
- [ ] Define safe triage, immediate protection, investigation, evidence,
  escalation, authority, law-enforcement or specialist involvement, and confidentiality.
- [ ] Use a victim-centred response and assess whether abrupt termination,
  reporting, public disclosure, or supplier exit could increase harm.
- [ ] Remediate harm and causes, including repayment of recruitment fees or wages,
  document return, safe employment, support, purchasing-practice change, and
  supplier-system improvement as appropriate and authorised.
- [ ] Verify remedy with affected workers or credible representatives where safe.
- [ ] Record recurrence, prevalence, overdue action, ineffective remediation,
  responsible owner, and escalation.

AI may organise supplier and evidence inventories or identify inconsistencies.
It must not interview workers without an approved safe protocol, determine that
exploitation occurred, report a person or supplier, or decide remediation alone.

- [ ] Track high-risk supply visibility, grievances, remedy quality, recurrence,

## 9. Statement and approval

- [ ] Use the legally required or officially supported reporting structure for
  each applicable jurisdiction and reporting period.
- [ ] Describe structure, operations, supply chains, policies, risks, due diligence,
  remediation, training, effectiveness, limitations, and planned action accurately.
- [ ] Report actual steps taken during the period; distinguish plans and prior-year activity.
- [ ] Do not claim the supply chain is “slavery-free” from policies, audits,
  questionnaires, incomplete visibility, or lack of identified cases.
- [ ] Obtain required governing-body approval and authorised signature.
- [ ] Publish, file, link, retain, and update the statement as required, ensuring
  it is accessible and consistent across group entities and jurisdictions.
