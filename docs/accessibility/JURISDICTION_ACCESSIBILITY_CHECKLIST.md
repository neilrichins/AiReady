# Jurisdiction accessibility checklist

Use this optional record to identify country, region, sector, procurement,
contractual, store, and organisational accessibility obligations for a website
or mobile application. It supplements technical evaluation; it is not legal
advice and does not determine which law applies.

Laws, regulations, designated standards, guidance, enforcement arrangements,
deadlines, and exceptions change. Verify every material conclusion against
current authoritative sources for the actual entity, users, market, service,
sector, contract, and release date. Obtain qualified legal or compliance review
where the conclusion affects a claim, exception, deadline, procurement,
regulated service, or release decision.

Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) may be incorporated into an
applicable requirement, but WCAG is not a substitute for determining the
governing law, standard, scope, or non-web software obligations.

## 1. Applicability discovery

- [ ] Identify every country and region where the provider is established, the product is offered, users are targeted, services are delivered, or procurement/public funding creates obligations.
- [ ] Identify parent, subsidiary, contracting, public-sector, educational, transport, financial, communications, audiovisual, e-commerce, publishing, health, employment, and other regulated roles.
- [ ] Determine whether the surface is a website, intranet, extranet, mobile website, native application, hybrid application, document, media, self-service function, consumer service, internal employee tool, or supporting service under each source.
- [ ] Check national, state/provincial, territorial, regional, municipal, sector, public-procurement, grant, licence, settlement, and contractual requirements.
- [ ] Identify obligations applying through a customer or contracting authority even when the developer is not directly regulated.
- [ ] Identify technical standards, exact versions, conformance levels, non-WCAG software requirements, platform requirements, and testing or monitoring methodologies incorporated by reference.
- [ ] Identify commencement and transition dates for new products, existing services, updates, contracts, organisation sizes, and special categories.
- [ ] Identify exclusions, exemptions, disproportionate/undue-burden or fundamental-alteration tests, microenterprise rules, archived or third-party content rules, and required assessments or alternatives.
- [ ] Identify duties beyond technical conformance: equal access, reasonable adjustments or accommodations, effective communication, support, procurement, training, feedback, complaints, enforcement, monitoring, reporting, records, and continuous maintenance.

## 4. Statements, user routes, and public information

- [ ] Determine whether an accessibility statement, conformance report, product accessibility information, procurement response, app-store disclosure, or service-provider information is required.
- [ ] Use the required format, model, language, location, publication channel, and update frequency.
- [ ] State the exact scope, standard/version, method, last evaluation date, compliance status, known non-accessible content, exceptions, disproportionate-burden decisions, alternatives, and remediation dates required by the applicable source.
- [ ] Provide an accessible feedback and request route, expected response process, alternative-format route, escalation or enforcement route, and support information.
- [ ] Ensure accessibility information and support are themselves accessible and available before purchase or download where required.
- [ ] Substantiate every public claim against the exact released product; do not publish “fully compliant” from a sample, automated scan, target, roadmap, or incomplete result matrix.

- [ ] Confirm the exception exists in the applicable source and that every precondition is met.
- [ ] Complete and retain the required assessment before relying on disproportionate burden, undue burden, unjustifiable hardship, fundamental alteration, microenterprise, legacy, archived-content, third-party, or other relief.
- [ ] Consider the resources and circumstances of the regulated entity rather than the convenience of one project or supplier.
- [ ] Provide accessible alternatives and equal/effective access where required; a workaround does not make the original barrier conformant.
- [ ] Record approval authority, duration, monitoring, remediation, change triggers, and required public disclosure.

## 6. Monitoring, complaints, and maintenance

- [ ] Define evaluation frequency, sampling, manual and automated methods, reporting, retention, and evidence requirements from the applicable source.
- [ ] Assign owners and response targets for accessibility feedback, alternative-format requests, complaints, regulator enquiries, procurement evidence, and incidents.
- [ ] Monitor content, code, dependencies, third parties, app-store builds, operating-system updates, assistive-technology updates, and legal or standards changes.
- [ ] Reassess after material product, market, entity, sector, contract, standard, enforcement, exception, or release changes.
- [ ] Preserve failed, partial, cancelled, remediated, and superseded evidence rather than rewriting history.
